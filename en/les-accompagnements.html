What holds us back

Why, despite wanting to change, does nothing really move?

You know something has to change. And yet, when it comes to acting, something holds you back. It is not necessarily a lack of willpower: roughly one intention in two never becomes real. Three mechanisms keep us in that in-between.

Too many options. Not enough clarity. Faced with an excess of possibilities, or when the next chapter is still blurred, momentum can freeze. As Barry Schwartz shows in The Paradox of Choice, when options multiply, our ability to decide can paradoxically shrink.

“I know this story no longer fits me. But I do not know where to start rewriting it.”

When the need for safety takes over from the need for change. Changing always involves some uncertainty, financial, professional, relational or simply emotional.

“What if I am wrong?” “What if I lose what I already have?” “Is this really the right moment?”

Little by little, the fear of losing what we know becomes stronger than the wish to discover what could exist, even if that means staying somewhere uncomfortable.

Read more

When doubt takes over from motivation or desire. A lack of confidence, the fear of failing or impostor syndrome can all stop us turning an intention into action.

“Am I really capable of this?” “Is it really worth it?” “Am I asking for too much?” “Will my family, my partner or my friends come with me?”

So we put it off. We look for one more piece of validation. We wait until we feel ready.

The investment

What does a life that no longer looks like you cost?

Misalignment costs energy, time and things given up. It also affects your health and your future. Through doubting, postponing and adapting, you end up drifting away from yourself without really meaning to.

The greatest risk is not investing in your transformation. It is continuing to pay the price of standing still.

You do not need more willpower. You need a space of your own to think, to choose and to act. Together we build what comes next, so that your life is not merely acceptable, but deeply right and fully alive.
